Home Loan for a New Flat

Documents, eligibility and EMI planning.

Updated 2026-11-28

Before you book

A loan decision depends on you and on the project. Ask your bank for an in-principle eligibility check before you pay a token, and ask whether the project is approved by the bank.

Documents banks usually ask for

  • Identity and address proof.
  • Income proof: salary slips and bank statements, or ITRs for the self-employed.
  • Bank statements for several months.
  • The booking letter or agreement and the cost sheet.
  • Details of existing loans and EMIs.

What affects eligibility

  • Monthly income and stability of employment.
  • Existing EMIs and credit card dues.
  • Credit score.
  • Age and loan tenure.
  • The share of the price you pay upfront.

How disbursement works on a new flat

For an under-construction flat the bank usually pays the developer in stages tied to construction progress. You pay interest only on the amount disbursed until the last stage, and then the full EMI starts. Ask your bank and the developer to align the payment schedule with the construction stages.

Test your numbers on the EMI calculator.

Practical tips

  • Compare offers from at least three lenders, including the processing fee.
  • Ask about prepayment charges on a floating-rate loan.
  • Do not borrow more than you can service through a rate rise.

Keep a reserve equal to several months of EMI, maintenance and rent, because a delay in possession means paying interest and rent together.

Questions for your bank

  1. What interest rate and processing fee do you offer for my profile?
  2. Is the project approved by your bank?
  3. How are disbursements linked to construction stages?
  4. What are the prepayment and foreclosure charges?
  5. Can I add a co-applicant to raise eligibility?

Keep your credit healthy

Avoid taking new loans or missing card payments in the months before you apply. A higher score can mean a lower rate, which over twenty years is worth far more than a small discount on the flat.
