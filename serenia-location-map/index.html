Before you visit

  • Use the live map to plan the route and note landmarks.
  • Visit at a weekday peak hour and on a weekend.
  • Check the access road and how it joins the main roads.

Reading a location honestly

A map shows where a project sits, not how it feels to live there. Check the roads at the hours you travel, find the nearest hospital, school and grocery store, and ask residents of nearby buildings about water supply, power cuts and traffic.

More: living in Kiwale.

Questions about the surroundings

  • Which roads will serve the project once all its towers are occupied?
  • Is public transport planned near the entrance?
  • What is being built on the adjacent plots?

A confident sales team can answer these with specifics. If an answer is vague, check the municipal plans or ask residents of nearby buildings.

A simple route test

Pick the three places you will go most often: your workplace, a school or hospital, and a market. Open each on the map from the project, set the departure time you would really leave, and note the journey time at a weekday morning, a weekday evening and a weekend. Do it again on the day you visit. A route that looks short on a map can be slow at peak time, and the reverse is also true, so measure instead of assuming.

Everyday needs

List what you need within a short drive: a clinic, a pharmacy, a grocery store, a school and a bank. Find each on the map and check that the road to it is not one that floods or jams. Our locality guides on this site list what to check for the area.
